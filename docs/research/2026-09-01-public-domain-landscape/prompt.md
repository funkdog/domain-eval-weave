# Research Brief: Selecting the First Authoritative Public Domain Landscape

## 1. Problem Frame

**We need to answer:**

Which public internet-software domain should DomainEval Weave adopt as its first substantial, reusable
Domain Landscape, and what exact primary sources, open-source implementations, public test vectors and
governance boundaries are required to make that Landscape authoritative enough for outside contributors?

Compare at least these candidates:

1. software supply-chain security and artifact provenance;
2. internet identity, authentication, federation and authorization;
3. payment lifecycle, funds movement, refunds, disputes, settlement and reconciliation;
4. public e-commerce after-sales/value-restoration.

The answer must select one initial Landscape or conclude that none is currently evidence-ready.

**Non-goals:**

- Do not design another small demo or one happy-path benchmark.
- Do not rank candidates by document count alone.
- Do not treat vendor marketing, tertiary tutorials or AI summaries as domain authority.
- Do not propose production-data ingestion.
- Do not assume one global canonical ontology or automatic truth ranking.
- Do not design a public Schema/API/CLI before the internal object boundaries are validated.

**Why now:**

DomainEval Weave has an internal Domain-First compiler proof covering Modules, exact Releases, typed
Mappings, Landscapes, bitemporal Claims, contextual Views, Product Contracts, Coverage, fail-closed
Evaluation Closures, Grade Atoms and independent qualification receipts. The current Commerce corpus
proves architecture but is repository-authored synthetic evidence. The next proof must be a complete,
publicly reproducible Domain Landscape built from official standards, regulations and real open-source
systems. It must provide a reliable contribution paradigm and baseline benchmark collection while
minimizing manual labeling and owner-confirmation cost.

## 2. Current Hypotheses

1. A public Landscape earns authority from traceable primary sources and plural implementations, not from
   the project authors' expertise.
2. Software supply-chain security may be the strongest first candidate because it combines formal
   specifications, regulation/government guidance, attestations, open-source implementations and
   executable artifacts.
3. Internet identity may be stronger because standards and conformance suites are mature, but scope and
   security risk may make a "complete" first Landscape too large.
4. E-commerce after-sales is understandable and commercially relevant, but cross-vendor rules and
   product-policy authority may remain proprietary or jurisdiction-specific.
5. Payments may offer a stronger balance of public authority, open-source implementations, business
   complexity and executable evidence than either supply-chain security or full e-commerce.
6. The project should automate extraction, reference checking, impact analysis and benchmark compilation,
   while preserving explicit human judgment only for scope, identity, authority conflicts and publication.
7. A useful baseline bench must be derived from Domain Claims and Closure semantics; it must not define the
   domain or hide unknown/unobservable areas.

**Evidence gaps:**

- We do not yet know which candidate has enough openly licensed primary evidence for a durable source pack.
- We do not know which candidate has genuinely independent open-source representations rather than many
  wrappers around one model.
- We do not know which official conformance/test-vector assets can be legally redistributed or only linked.
- We do not know where source authority remains ambiguous enough to require high manual confirmation cost.
- We do not know whether public regulations add useful normative plurality or mainly introduce unstable
  legal interpretation.

These are hypotheses, not conclusions. Try to falsify them.

## 3. Disconfirm First

Before supporting a candidate:

1. Find evidence that its apparently authoritative source base is incomplete, vendor-controlled,
   non-redistributable, stale or mutually inconsistent.
2. Find successful public-domain modeling or conformance ecosystems that use a different approach.
3. Identify domains where formal standards exist but real implementations intentionally diverge.
4. Identify benchmark contamination, conformance gaming or "test suite defines truth" failures.
5. Explain why an attractive candidate might still impose too much expert review, legal interpretation or
   maintenance burden for an open contributor community.
6. Look for evidence that a smaller or different domain would create a stronger reusable paradigm.

## 4. Source Mix Quota

Use primary sources only for claims that affect the decision.

Required source categories:

- standards/specifications from their issuing bodies;
- laws, regulations or official government guidance where normatively relevant;
- official documentation and design records from real open-source projects;
- official conformance suites, test vectors, schemas or datasets;
- first-party security advisories or implementation divergence records;
- licensing terms for every source or test asset proposed for inclusion.

For each source record:

- direct URL and issuing organization;
- source type and authority scope;
- version/date and current status;
- license/redistribution constraints;
- which Domain Module/Claim family it could support;
- whether we can snapshot bytes, must link only, or need a derived CC0 fixture;
- conflicts or non-substitutability with other sources.

Commercial vendor docs may support vendor-scoped Claims only. They cannot establish universal standards,
legal duties or cross-provider truth.

## 5. Local Constraints

The recommendation must fit these constraints:

- Git-native, offline-first and content-addressed.
- Knowledge and decisions remain in repository artifacts, not a graph database or hosted registry.
- Only synthetic Episodes and observations; no production user data.
- Public code/docs are Apache-2.0; repository-authored synthetic sources are CC0.
- Source licenses and provenance must be bound to artifact identity.
- Domain truth is a typed graph with plural representations, not a directory taxonomy.
- Claims separately encode kind, epistemic state, observability, applicability, valid time and recorded
  time.
- Source Authority is scoped evidence metadata, never an automatic global rank.
- Dependency direction is Domain → View → Product Contract → Projection → Closure → Grader/Run.
- Missing, unknown, contested, unobservable and out-of-scope are distinct.
- Hard verdicts require complete identity, applicability and observation semantics; otherwise Closure must
  refuse.
- The public contribution journey must minimize manual labels and confirmations, but never automate
  semantic identity, authority conflict resolution or publication by pretending uncertainty does not exist.
- Existing internal architecture acceptance includes exact Releases/Mappings, competing representations,
  scoped authority, evolution impact, bitemporal replay, Coverage anti-truncation, successful/refused
  Closures, atomic grades and artifact-only replay.
- Recent review lessons: full source metadata must enter digests; upstream compilers cannot read downstream
  contracts; governance lists and impact reports must be mechanically checked; independent qualification
  requires runtime-verifiable authority, unseen observation vectors, freeze/execution/unseal receipts and
  replayable Runs.

Do not collapse this into one unexplained total score.

### B. Recommended Landscape boundary

For the recommended candidate provide:

- Scope Declaration and explicit exclusions;
- proposed Model Contexts/Domain Modules;
- important Concepts, lifecycle/events and identity boundaries;
- competing local representations that must not be merged;
- typed cross-module Mappings/Bridges;
- Claim families and applicability dimensions;
- Source Authority classes and non-substitution rules;
- versioning/evolution scenarios;
- contextual Views and any Product/Policy Contracts;
- known unknown, contested and unobservable areas.

### C. Primary-source inventory

At least 12 high-value sources across multiple issuing authorities, each with provenance, status, license,
authority scope and proposed use. Quality matters more than count.

### D. Open-source implementation inventory

At least three independently maintained implementations or toolchains. Explain where their models agree,
diverge or translate.

### E. Baseline bench design

Propose a non-trivial benchmark family derived from the domain:

- Grade Atoms and target Claims;
- Gold/equivalent/mutant strategy;
- public validation vs independently sealed qualification;
- observation/absence semantics;
- one complete Closure and at least two honest refusal modes;
- reusable test vectors and redistribution constraints;
- anti-overfitting and contribution-admission process.

### F. Low-human-cost contribution workflow

Separate what can be automated from what requires judgment:

| Step | Automatable evidence work | Required human judgment | Failure if automated |
|---|---|---|---|

Include proposed tooling for source ingestion, source updates, extraction proposals, reference checking,
conflict surfacing, mapping suggestions, impact propagation, Coverage and benchmark compilation.

### G. Onboarding pain forecast

Predict concrete friction a new contributor/operator will encounter:

- evidence discovery/licensing;
- scope and language boundaries;
- cross-project identity;
- authority conflicts;
- temporal/version divergence;
- observation gaps;
- benchmark qualification;
- maintenance and review load.

For each pain propose an experiment that measures it without reducing everything to one score.

### H. Evidence tables

Provide separate tables for evidence supporting and opposing each hypothesis, with confidence and direct
primary-source links.

### I. Confidence summary

Mark each hypothesis supported, opposed or unresolved and explain why.

## 7. Decision Interface

Every major finding must map to:

- **Adopt:** sufficiently evidenced for the first public Landscape.
- **Pilot:** promising but requires a bounded source/license or implementation experiment.
- **Defer:** too proprietary, legally ambiguous, broad, unstable or manual today.

End with one decision packet:

- recommended domain;
- reason it is the strongest authority/contribution proof;
- minimum viable but non-trivial scope;
- evidence and license blockers;
- first implementation slice;
- stop conditions that would invalidate the choice;
- what must be proven before any public Schema/API/CLI promotion.

## 8. Risk Register

At minimum assess:

1. Standards-conformance bias: official specs may not describe production reality.
2. Vendor authority leakage: vendor docs may be mistaken for universal truth.
3. Legal interpretation risk: regulations may require jurisdiction-qualified Claims and expert review.
4. Benchmark capture: visible tests may become the de facto domain and encourage gaming.
5. Licensing contamination: source/test assets may not be redistributable.
6. Automation theater: extraction confidence may hide unresolved identity or authority judgment.
7. Domain-lake growth: immutable versions may create an unmaintainable active surface.
8. Contributor burden: "complete" may become so expensive that nobody contributes.

For each risk provide falsifiers, mitigation and an owner.
